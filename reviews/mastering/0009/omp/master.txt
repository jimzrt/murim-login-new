# Chapter 9

Ding.

> **System**
>
> - You have acquired **Jin Family’s Manoeuvre Technique**.
>
> - You can obtain various effects by mastering this martial art.
>
> - You have completed the achievement **Learn a Martial Art**.
>
> - As a reward for completing the achievement, you have acquired the title **Novice Trainee**.

“Good grief.”

The moment the System notification appeared, I slumped to the floor. Dust billowed into the air, but who cared? I already looked like a beggar anyway.

*Why is this so damn hard?*

There were only two kinds of books in Jin Taekyung’s study.

Erotic novels. And books that weren’t erotic novels.

Of the more than four hundred books, only about a hundred remained once I removed the erotic novels.

*What an incredible bastard.*

If he’d been born in Korea, he would’ve run an illegal adult website. If he’d been born in America, he would’ve ended up in prison.

At any rate, once I finished sorting them, there were only about thirty martial arts manuals.

*If only he’d collected half as many manuals as erotic novels.*

It was a shame as far as I was concerned, but I had still found something worthwhile: the martial arts manuals I needed right now.

The Jin Family’s Spear Technique and the Jin Family’s Manoeuvre Technique. They were hereditary martial arts of the Jin Family of Taiyuan—arts I’d supposedly forgotten after going so long without training.

*Check the Jin Family’s Manoeuvre Technique.*

Ding.

> **System**
>
> Skill Window
>
> **Jin Family’s Manoeuvre Technique**
>
> **Type:** Manoeuvre Technique
>
> **Grade:** First Rate
>
> **Restriction:** Direct descendants of the Jin Family of Taiyuan
>
> **Realm:** First Stage
>
> **Description:** A manoeuvre technique devised by the founder of the Jin Family of Taiyuan. It has few variations and is monotonous, but practical.

The description of the Jin Family’s Spear Technique wasn’t much different. Except…

*“Few variations and monotonous,” my ass.*

This hereditary martial art, supposedly created by the family founder—or was it some prehistoric bird?—was complicated as hell. Just thinking about last night made me grind my teeth.

> **System**
>
> - Acquisition of **Jin Family’s Manoeuvre Technique** will now begin.
>
> - Memorizing the formula. Speed varies according to the martial art’s Grade and your Intelligence.

Right. Everything had been fine up to that point.

The problem came next.

> **System**
>
> - Displaying the movement sequence for **Jin Family’s Manoeuvre Technique**. (0 / 100)

I practiced the manoeuvre technique by following the footprints displayed by the System, but it was absurdly strict and exacting. Stray even slightly outside the marked footprints and I failed. Take too long to make the next move and I failed.

I had to follow the entire sequence perfectly for it to count as one completion.

*What a fucking garbage game.*

There was a major difference between my real-world physique and this character’s. I was nearly half a handspan shorter, and my reach was shorter too. I couldn’t make the fine adjustments I needed, so one mistake followed another.

*It’s a miracle I managed it at all.*

By the time I completed the sequence one hundred times, my hands and feet were trembling.

*Still, I gained one thing.*

After years as a Hunter, I was thoroughly accustomed to combat—especially fighting groups. In a life-or-death battle, the two most important things were luck first and your feet second.

Advance. Retreat. Or stop.

Even if an arm was cut off, you could survive as long as your legs still moved. But if your legs were cut off, it was over.

Your feet had to move before your arms could. That was how every battle I’d fought had worked, and that was why I’d chosen to learn the manoeuvre technique first.

*But…*

It was too slow. Even with the System’s help, more than half a day had flown by. How much longer would I need to learn the spear technique too?

*Today makes five days.*

I’d heard that time passed faster in virtual-reality games than in the real world. But even after accounting for the time multiplier, five days was a long time.

“Whew.”

I sighed and forced the distracting thoughts from my mind.

Rescue? Logout?

This wasn’t the time to cling to possibilities. I had to keep moving forward, even if I had to do it alone.

*That’s enough for today.*

I gathered up the martial arts manuals and stood.

When I reached the bedroom on the second floor, I realized that one unusual book had gotten mixed in among them.

**The Night King: Well-Endowed Man**

“Ahem.”

Oh dear. What a mistake.

* * *

“Are you up already?”

The servant stared at me with wide eyes, holding a tray laden with breakfast.

“I couldn’t sleep…”

It was the truth. Circulating my qi was the reason.

*This is ridiculously effective.*

Circulating my qi didn’t just clear my mind and sharpen my senses. It also relieved fatigue. Come to think of it, I didn’t think I’d felt tired once since I started doing it.

*The pain is almost gone too.*

“Your wounds could worsen, so please don’t push yourself too hard.”

Warmth spread through my chest at the servant’s words.

*Even this shitty game has an NPC who’s like a ray of light. I’ve finally met a normal person.*

*Why is something this small getting to me?*

Choked up, I began eating breakfast.

Every side dish was either too salty or too bland, but hunger made them tasty enough. I polished off the meal, then downed the bowl of herbal medicine in one gulp.

“Ugh.”

The taste made my face scrunch up. Once the servant had cleared everything away, he bowed his head.

“Then I shall take my leave.”

“Ah, wait a second.”

“Please speak less formally. Why are you using honorifics with me?”

Come to think of it, he had a point.

At first, the graphics and artificial intelligence had been so realistic that I’d spoken politely to every NPC I met. By now, though, I’d grown somewhat accustomed to them.

At last, the time had come to reclaim my dignity as a user.

I answered sternly.

“For now, I’ll speak however I’m comfortable.”

*Fuck. I can’t bring myself to speak informally.*

I couldn’t exactly talk down to a man who was clearly over forty, throwing around “you punk” and “you bastard.”

*Damn game. The graphics are so good I can’t even speak casually.*

*At this rate, I’ll end up becoming friends with an NPC.*

When I emphasized that I’d injured my head, the servant reluctantly nodded.

“I suppose it can’t be helped. Is there something you require?”

“I was wondering if there was an empty room.”

The servant tilted his head.

“I can arrange one for you, but what might you need it for?”

“I want to practice martial arts.”

“Pardon?”

“All the rooms here are either dirty or stuffy… Why do you look like that?”

* * *

After leaving the pavilion, the servant ran straight to the Family Head’s office.

Once his report was finished, Jin Wikyung’s solemn voice rang out from beyond a tower of documents.

“You’ve done well.”

The instant the servant left, Jin Wikyung sprang to his feet and sent the documents flying into the air.

“This calls for a celebration! I’m not working today!”

“And who gave you permission?”

Wipeng caught every last fluttering page and sighed.

“If anyone else finds out you’re behaving like this, they’ll start talking again.”

“The Third Young Master says he’s finally going to train in martial arts in earnest. What could possibly be more important than that?”

“The fact that the Elder Council is waiting to pounce on you.”

At the words *Elder Council*, Jin Wikyung’s expression darkened.

“Damn old men.”

“A family council meeting may be called soon. From their perspective, they’ve spotted an opening. Of course they’ll sink their teeth into it.”

The agenda was obvious. It would begin with Jin Taekyung’s usual conduct and end with an attack on Jin Wikyung himself, the Lesser Family Head.

“Those bastards are persistent.”

“Is that anything new? Old pillars always end up crawling with bugs.”

“Isn’t there any way around it?”

“So you’re volunteering to serve as the Third Young Master’s shield yet again.”

Wipeng sighed.

“My lord, may I offer a word?”

“I refuse.”

“Then I refuse your refusal. This is the fifth time he’s embezzled the family’s public funds. I’ve lost count of everything else. If we had enforced the family rules properly, it would be a miracle that the Third Young Master was still alive.”

“Now, now.”

“Since we’re on the subject, grab anyone in the family and ask them. To you, he’s your beloved little brother. To everyone else…”

Wipeng shook his head.

“Honestly, I can’t even say it.”

“Do you have something against our youngest? What’s with that tone?”

“I’m frustrated. That’s all. Frustrated. The Third Young Master causes trouble, you clean it up, and then you agree to the Elder Council’s demands to protect him. They’re chipping away at your authority bit by bit. Do you know what rumor is going around these days?”

“What rumor?”

“Some people say the Third Young Master belongs to the Elder faction. That the Elder Council gives him spending money and he deliberately causes trouble for you.”

Jin Wikyung’s eyelids began to twitch.

“That’s an outrage!”

“I’d rather it were true. If the Elder Council slipped him a few silver coins, he wouldn’t have to embezzle the family’s funds.”

“You…”

“I’ve said my piece. Fire me if you want.”

Jin Wikyung groaned and let out a deep sigh.

“I’ve been thinking this can’t go on much longer either.”

“Thinking is all well and good. The problem is that you never act on it.”

“Even so, I should do everything I can this time.”

“Oh, my lord…”

“This will be the last time. I give you my word.”

Jin Wikyung’s expression turned serious. Wipeng asked suspiciously,

“Do you really mean that?”

“He’s still a child whose memory hasn’t fully returned. Besides… he changed into an entirely different person overnight. You’ve noticed it too.”

“That’s true, but…”

Wipeng trailed off.

The Third Young Master had certainly changed. Whether his memory loss was real or a lie, his current behavior was undeniably encouraging.

After a moment’s thought, Jin Wikyung spoke.

“Wipeng.”

“Yes.”

“Prepare an order in my name.”

“What sort of order…?”

“Find a few suitable charges and sentence him to indefinite seclusion in the training hall.”

“Ah.”

Wipeng slapped his forehead.

It was largely for show, but under the circumstances, it was an excellent emergency measure. It would ease the pressure they faced at the upcoming family council meeting while reducing the severity of Jin Taekyung’s eventual punishment.

And on top of that…

“It also fulfills the Third Young Master’s request. He was looking for somewhere to train.”

Wasn’t this three birds with one stone? Wipeng was genuinely impressed.

“As expected of you, my lord.”

“That’s how the people in my family are. Oh, did I ever tell you? Taekyung was such a clever child when he was young. One day…”

“…I’ll go draft the order.”

* * *

“Therefore, for violating fourteen regulations and disrupting the discipline of the family, the Third Young Master, Jin Taekyung, is hereby sentenced to indefinite seclusion in the training hall.”

His name was… Wipeng, wasn’t it?

I listened silently to the sour-looking bastard, then raised my hand.

“I have a question.”

“Go ahead.”

“What does ‘indefinite’ mean?”

Wipeng answered reluctantly.

“It means there is no set end date.”

“Oh.”

I’d thought the game’s language system had malfunctioned. Fortunately, it meant exactly what I thought it meant.

“Ha ha ha.”

“Ho ho ho.”

Laughter was contagious. The warriors who had accompanied Wipeng began laughing along with me.

With the atmosphere now nice and warm, Wipeng read the final line.

“The criminal Jin Taekyung shall submit to his bonds.”

“No.”

“…”

“…”

“I said no. Fuck.”

The two men approaching with rope restraints looked at Wipeng as if to say, *This isn’t how it was supposed to go.*

I ignored them and said what I had to say.

“I asked you to find me a room to practice in, not throw me in prison.”

Were these people all completely fucking insane?

“Now, Third Young Master. Calm down and listen to me.”

“Listen to what, for fuck’s sake? You’re going to tell me the training hall has everything I need for practice and the living conditions aren’t bad. That kind of bullshit.”

Judging by Wipeng’s expression, I’d hit the nail on the head. I drove the point home.

“You people are the type to tell someone to enjoy military service because soldiers got a pay raise. Forget it. I’m not going in. I’ll practice by myself in my room or out in the yard.”

Honestly, at first glance, the training hall didn’t sound so bad.

But the word *indefinite* stuck in my mind like a thorn. I urgently needed to learn martial arts and Level up. I couldn’t spend every day in the training hall craning my neck and waiting for someone to let me out.

I flopped onto the floor.

“Go ahead and gut me!”

“Third Young Master, that’s enough. Please get up.”

Wipeng scowled at me.

“The Lesser Family Head made this decision entirely for your sake.”

“Jin Wikyung—I mean, my brother?”

That brother-obsessed idiot had given this order?

At that moment, Wipeng’s lips moved. A voice reached my ears at the same time, with a strange quality unlike ordinary speech.

> This is Sound Transmission. Don’t be alarmed. Just listen.

Sound Transmission. I remembered reading about it in martial arts novels. It was a kind of telepathy that only masters could use.

> You may not know this because you’ve lost your memory, but the Third Young Master is considered a person of concern. A harsher punishment may be imposed soon, so the Lesser Family Head is acting before that happens.

I’d worked hard and lived an honest life for twenty-seven years. How had I ended up facing an even harsher punishment?

As I wallowed in sorrow, Wipeng’s Sound Transmission continued in my ear.

> It may be called indefinite seclusion, but do you really think the Lesser Family Head intends to leave you in the training hall for the rest of your life?

I shook my head. There was no way he would do that.

Unless he wanted the two of us locked in there together.

> I’ll get you out within seven days and nights at the latest. How does that sound?

Fierce determination burned in Wipeng’s eyes. If I refused again, he looked ready to beat me senseless and drag me there himself.

*Fuck, are all the NPCs here thugs or what?*

*Hey, asshole. Are you really that good at fighting?*

I focused my Qi Sense and checked Wipeng’s Level.

> **System**
>
> **Lv. ???**

“…”

*He really is a thug.*

A Level thug.

He was probably every bit as much of a human butcher as Jin Wikyung.

Wipeng’s eyes widened as he asked,

> What will you do?

Trembling with fear, I held up three fingers.

> …You want me to get you out in three days?

*What kind of bastard is this?*

Wipeng glared at me as if he were thinking exactly that. Then he sighed.

“Escort him.”
