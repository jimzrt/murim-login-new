# Chapter 10

After walking for more than ten minutes, we reached the cliff that sealed off the rear of the Jin Family of Taiyuan estate. A vast cavern gaped open in its face, and one man stood before it.

“Hmm. You’re here.”

He had the build of a brown bear and a solemn way of speaking.

Jin Wikyung.

I bowed awkwardly.

“Hello, big… brother.”

Jin Wikyung made me uncomfortable. As if suddenly acquiring an NPC family wasn’t enough, he took an absurd amount of interest in me.

Just look at him. He was trying his damnedest not to show it with everyone watching. But if you looked closely, his eyes were glistening.

*Is this guy seriously that sentimental?*

He looked like he could make a triad boss back down, yet somehow every character in this game was completely bizarre.

“I can no longer overlook your conduct. In my capacity as Lesser Family Head and acting Family Head, I have ordered you confined to the training hall. Do you have anything to say?”

*Of course I do.*

But this was all staged. On the way here, Wipeng had explained what was really going on. There was a power struggle among the Jin Family of Taiyuan’s upper ranks, and Jin Wikyung was shielding me from it.

In other words, this forced confinement was all for show.

*You needed somewhere to train anyway, didn’t you, Young Master? Just endure it for three days.*

Remembering Wipeng’s final words, I bowed my head and pretended to repent.

“I will gladly accept my punishment.”

The best thing about this play was how short the lines were.

Jin Wikyung delivered his final line with sorrow in his eyes.

“Confine the criminal to the training hall. His release date will be announced later.”

The moment he finished speaking, two warriors guarding the entrance approached and seized me by the arms.

The play was over. Time to exit the stage.

I stopped at the entrance to the training hall.

*I’ve had everything you need brought inside. Don’t push yourself too hard, little brother.*

With Jin Wikyung’s Sound Transmission in my ear, I took my first step inside.

* * *

The training hall was, in a word, a cave. An artificial cave carved into the cliff, at that.

It was tall, spacious, and damp.

Splash. Splash.

How long had I been following the warrior assigned to the training hall? By the time I could no longer tell whether I was wearing leather shoes or wet mops, a massive iron gate appeared before me.

*Wow…*

My mouth fell open the moment I saw it. The gate filled the passage without leaving a single gap. It looked less like a door than a wall built to prevent anyone from entering or leaving.

The warrior guiding me raised his torch and shouted, “Open the gate!”

Grrrnnng—

The enormous iron gate slowly opened its jaws.

It wasn’t some magic command like “Open, Sesame.” An NPC waiting nearby had simply pulled a protruding lever that operated the gate.

Even so, the sight was overwhelming.

And the moment I saw what lay beyond it—

“Whoa.”

This time, I couldn’t hold back my amazement.

When I first entered the training hall, I had imagined a dank underground cavern, but…

“What is all this?”

There was a broad bed with a fur blanket so inviting that merely looking at it lifted my spirits. Instead of a damp, uneven stone floor, a smooth gray surface stretched before me.

*Cement… Obviously not. Limestone, maybe?*

Aside from the chill in the air—and even with that taken into account—the place was far better than I had imagined.

*This is supposed to be punishment?*

As I stared around in bewilderment, someone cleared his throat behind me. I turned to find the warrior who had guided me here.

“We’ve prepared everything you need. I’ll be going now.”

Grrrnnng.

As I watched the iron gate slowly close, I suddenly remembered the Sound Transmission I had heard at the entrance.

*I’ve had everything you need brought inside. Don’t push yourself too hard, little brother.*

Ah, the devotion of an NPC who cared for his little brother.

This pathetic user was moved to tears.

* * *

It happened all the time in the real world.

High-ranking officials accused of corruption would show up at the prosecutors’ office in wheelchairs or check themselves into private hospital suites to avoid an investigation.

My situation was a little different, but the general picture was the same. Deeply moved, I gazed upon my private suite in the training hall.

“So this is the life of a gold spoon.”[^1]

It wasn’t just the graphics and artificial intelligence that were realistic. The game even conveyed the social message that no matter how hard anyone else tried, being born with a gold spoon was best.

God, what would I have done if I hadn’t gotten this character? His father was the Family Head, his eldest brother was the Lesser Family Head, and his second brother was a martial arts prodigy. No wonder he could spend all his time loafing around pleasure houses without a care in the world.

*Jin Taekyung, you bastard…*

Wasn’t he actually a great sage who had grasped the ways of the world at a tender age?

Shuddering at the realistic God-Spoon System, I explored the training hall, which covered well over three thousand square feet. Before long, I found the “necessities” the warrior had mentioned.

*Food first.*

The food was divided between two jars. When I looked inside, I found fist-sized balls giving off a powerful medicinal scent.

> **System**
>
> **Item Window**
>
> **Excellent Grain-Repelling Pill**
>
> **Type:** Pill
>
> **Grade:** First Rate
>
> **Restriction:** None
>
> **Description:** A grain-repelling pill made by crudely cramming in all kinds of beneficial medicinal ingredients. Restores vitality when consumed and grants additional effects when taken over an extended period.

“Oh, so this is the famous grain-repelling pill?”

I had seen these plenty of times in martial arts novels. They were light, compact, easy to carry, and supposedly provided all the nutrition you needed.

The other jar was packed full of them too.

*I should put these in my inventory for now.*

I took hold of both jars and muttered, “Acquire item.”

If anyone else had seen me, they would have fallen over in shock. The two jars sitting there a moment ago vanished as if they had evaporated.

I gazed fondly at the jars now stored in my inventory.

*That takes care of food.*

The second necessity was water. Since the training hall was, after all, a cave, a cold underground spring in one corner took care of my drinking needs.

Then came the third and final necessity.

“Hmm.”

Several weapons hung in a neat row on a rack. Naturally, the first one I picked up was a sturdy-looking wooden spear.

*Appraise item.*

Ding.

> **System**
>
> **Item Window**
>
> **Training Wooden Spear**
>
> **Type:** Weapon
>
> **Grade:** Third Rate
>
> **Restriction:** None
>
> **Description:** Made for beginners.

“Oh.”

A wooden training spear made for beginners. It was exactly what I needed right now.

All I needed was one more thing.

“Open inventory.”

Grinning, I pulled out the martial arts manual titled *Jin Family’s Spear Technique*.

Ding.

> **System**
>
> - Would you like to learn the Jin Family’s Spear Technique? (3 / 10)

“Obviously.”

The System was great.

Sometimes, it was even adorable.

* * *

While practicing the Jin Family’s Manoeuvre Technique yesterday, I had learned something for the first time.

Ding wasn’t the System’s only notification sound.

And I had learned just how much I hated the other one.

Beep!

> **System**
>
> - The movement failed.
>
> - Successful attempts: 2 / 100

The failure message. A System window that appeared without fail whenever I botched a movement.

I had lost count of how many times I’d seen it.

I stared at the *Training Wooden Spear* in my hand.

*I got this all wrong.*

Maybe because it was made of wood, the spear was light. Whether I thrust or swung it, it moved quickly.

That was the problem.

*It’s too light to control.*

Its lightness made fine adjustments difficult, so I kept slipping up. There was no way this damned picky System would overlook even the smallest mistake.

Beep.

> **System**
>
> - The movement failed.
>
> - Successful attempts: 5 / 100

“Ah, fuck.”

In the end, I tossed the *Training Wooden Spear* aside and pulled the *Sharp Spear* from my inventory. Its length and the thickness of its shaft were roughly the same as the spear I had used in the real world.

So why hadn’t I taken it out from the start?

“It’s fucking heavy. Seriously.”

Since it was made entirely of solid steel, its weight was no joke. By feel alone, the monster had to weigh close to fifty kilograms.

My Stamina wouldn’t last if I swung something like this for hours.

My current realm was Second Rate. Even with the System’s help, I couldn’t whirl around more than half a sack of rice as if it weighed nothing.

*It’s not like I can suddenly tap into the strength of a tiger.*

Then it hit me.

“…Huh?”

What had I just said?

The strength of a tiger?

“I do have it.”

This was a game. It had a System and stats.

And I had internal energy.

Ten years of internal energy that I could draw out through a cultivation technique!

It was embarrassing that I had forgotten, even for a moment.

“It’s not like I’ve ever used anything like that before…”

They said experience was the best teacher. There was a reason an F-rank Hunter was F-rank. With barely any mana to speak of, I had always made do with my bare body. Even among Hunters, I was treated like I was only half a Hunter.

*Still, that solves one problem.*

I let out a disbelieving laugh and picked up the spear. Then, slowly and carefully, I drew out my internal energy.

The formula of the Jin Family’s Cultivation Technique, imprinted in my mind by the System, raced through my thoughts and guided my internal energy along its prescribed path.

A prickling sensation ran through me.

The response was immediate.

The ten years of internal energy coiled in my dantian spread throughout my body. Because this was a game and I was a martial artist, I could feel that energy flowing into every part of me.

*This is…*

Power surged through my entire body. My vastly heightened physical abilities and senses once again brought exhilaration to someone who had spent his life as an F-rank Hunter.

*A person can change this much?*

I gripped the spear and began practicing the *Jin Family’s Spear Technique*. The fifty-kilogram iron spear no longer felt heavy. It thrust and slashed through the air exactly as I willed it.

Before long—

Ding.

> **System**
>
> - Successful attempts: 6 / 100

The notification I had been waiting for finally began to ring.

* * *

Jin Wikyung spoke with worry etched across his face.

“He’s doing all right, isn’t he?”

“He should be, if he has any sense of shame.”

“He still hasn’t fully recovered… He’ll be all right, won’t he?”

“Anyone who didn’t know better would think the Third Young Master was on death’s door. At this point, even a little spit would cure him.”

“No. You only say that because you don’t know how frail the youngest has been since childhood.”

Wipeng stared at him incredulously.

“The elixirs and tonics that have gone into the Third Young Master would fill an entire room. Have you already forgotten the hundred-year snow ginseng theft last year?”

“Ahem. That was…”

“The Medicine King Hall Master was so furious that he ran around threatening to cut open the Third Young Master’s stomach. To be honest, even as I restrained him, I couldn’t help thinking that cutting him open would count as self-defense.”

Jin Wikyung quietly averted his gaze.

The matter had ultimately been settled by compensating the Medicine King Hall out of Jin Wikyung’s personal fortune, but the Hall Master’s fury at the time had been extraordinary.

“After swallowing an elixir like that, the Third Young Master probably won’t suffer so much as a minor illness for the rest of his life.”

“It still wasn’t enough. Can’t you tell just by looking at him? My heart aches every time I see the youngest. He’s nothing but a skeleton with a few scraps of flesh clinging to it. Every morning, he looks so feeble and drained of strength.”

“Drained of strength?”

Wipeng suddenly remembered a rumor he had once heard.

Among the courtesans of Taiyuan’s red-light district, Jin Taekyung was supposedly known as the Night King.

*Just how impressive is he?*

It seemed the medicine had done its job in at least one respect. Without realizing it, Wipeng raised his forearm and tried to imagine the size.

“What are you doing?”

“Ah, nothing.”

Jin Wikyung sighed as he looked at the mountain of documents piled before him.

“Between the youngest and everything happening inside and outside the family, there’s no end to my worries. Especially… I don’t like that ‘they’ have made contact.”

“You mean the Mount Heng Sword Sect.”

The Mount Heng Sword Sect.

That name carried considerable weight.

Since an undefeated wandering martial artist first hung its signboard decades ago, the sect had grown at a frightening pace. Now, it had become powerful enough to threaten the Jin Family of Taiyuan’s position.

“What could they want?”

“I’ve sent my subordinates to investigate.”

Jin Wikyung toyed with the letter from the Mount Heng Sword Sect.

Why were they coming? What did they want?

One question led to another, until he arrived at a single conclusion.

“Notify every branch in Shanxi. Whatever the Mount Heng Sword Sect’s purpose, tell them to make every possible preparation.”

This was Murim.

Only those who were prepared would survive to see tomorrow.

[^1]: In Korean, “gold spoon” is shorthand for someone born into wealth; “God-Spoon” is a pun that escalates the expression.
