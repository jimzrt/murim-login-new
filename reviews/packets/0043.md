# Chapter 43

Swish.

A sharpened dagger dug into the corpse of a Hobgoblin.

Body fluids splattered, and a foul stench filled the air, but I didn’t stop. I discarded the heads, neatly cut away the hides from their backs and bellies, and stacked them one after another.

“You’re quite skilled.”

“…Thank you.”

It was Team Leader Choi. His kind tone made me feel guilty. How childish was I, getting jealous of someone like him?

Especially after dragging up an illusion I’d already decided to forget.

*I’m not going through puberty.*

In the end, I finished the job with my face burning red.

It took a little over ten minutes, so about twenty seconds per corpse? Im Kkeokjeong, who had quietly approached at my speed, even gaped.

“Damn, did you do nothing but this after you ate?”

“There are plenty of people who can do this much.”

“Plenty, my ass. You’re a total professional.”

Hmm. That made me a little proud.

I’d tried to be modest, but Im Kkeokjeong was right. There weren’t many Hunters who could butcher monsters this cleanly at this speed.

“I’m just fast with my hands.”

I was the kind of guy who’d worked part-time at factories during school and even mastered sticking eyes onto dolls. In a way, this was something I’d started before I began working as a Hunter.

“I kept doing it during raids, too.”

“You switched careers completely?”

“No. I just did both. A side job.”

“A side job?”

Team Leader Choi, who had been listening quietly, suddenly joined the conversation.

“Oh, yes.”

“Do you receive additional pay?”

“Of course. It’s work, too.”

“How long have you been doing it?”

Im Kkeokjeong clicked his tongue.

“You’re one tough bastard. I couldn’t do that.”

“You get used to it. It also helps build your stamina.”

“Your life’s on the line, and you’re building stamina? Hahaha. Team Leader Choi, didn’t I tell you? There’s nobody who works as doggedly as this kid.”

*Did you say that?*

“Hm.”

Instead of answering, Team Leader Choi stared at me for a moment, then turned away.

What was that reaction supposed to mean? While I stood there bewildered, Im Kkeokjeong gave me a mysterious smile.

“Keep working hard.”

*What was that supposed to mean?*

The raid proceeded smoothly. The veteran E-rank Hunters had worked together for so long that they moved like a single body. As they reduced the monsters’ numbers, Team Leader Choi took over.

Slash—

Whatever I wanted to do later, the raid came first.

That was basic courtesy to my teammates—and the principle that would keep me alive.

Team Leader Choi and Im Kkeokjeong hovered nearby throughout the entire rest period, as if they weren’t tired at all.

Slice. Slice. Slice.

*Save me.*

I sent a distress signal to Team Leader Choi, who was sitting nearby, but he answered by quietly slipping away.

Team Leader Choi had contributed the most, so he would probably take about half. But with so few people, the other team members would still make good money.

Of course, not me. According to my contract, the 300,000-won base pay was all I would receive.

Team Leader Choi was a fairly generous employer. He was a C-rank Hunter way out of my league, yet he always spoke politely. His personality was a little unusual, but by the standards of this line of work, someone like him was a decent human being.

*And he’s a skilled Hunter I can trust.*

Thanks to him, we’d made it this far at a steady, rapid pace.

All that remained was the final part of the Gate.

The Boss Zone.

If we opened the stone gate in front of us, the Gate’s boss monster and the mana field that would send us outside should be waiting beyond it.

Who talked about food right before entering a Boss Zone? And on top of that, there was someone planning to order sushi and eat by himself.

*Is it really okay to be this relaxed?*

Of course, I had no right to say that. Just carrying the bags and butchering monsters here and there had already earned me a day’s pay.

I hoped Team Leader Choi would call me often until I found a job at a new Guild. I could do a sweet part-time job like this a hundred times over.

“Yes.”

Boom!

It was there.

An old Hobgoblin wearing robes covered in strange patterns. It held a dried-up staff in one hand and a sharp dagger in the other.

—Karruk. Chwi. Akto.

Despite the loud crash of the stone gate breaking apart, it continued muttering something. Whenever a syllable ended, black energy rose from the corpses scattered around us.

*Wait. Corpses?*

I had seen correctly. Nearly a hundred Hobgoblins lay dead on the altar. Looking closer, they were the very monsters we were supposed to fight in the Boss Zone.

*What the hell is this?*

Everyone, myself included, froze at the situation none of us had ever encountered before.

Then Team Leader Choi shouted like a thunderbolt.

“Get outside. Now!”

That was when the unknown old Hobgoblin turned its head.

Clink.

Its staff shook lightly.

—Mita. Allo.

The air trembled.

Magic was being activated.

“Behind the shields!”

The tanks hurriedly raised their tower shields. But that was the wrong decision. What the Hobgoblin had used wasn’t an attack spell.

Boom. Boom. Boom!

“It’s behind us! The passage is being blocked!”

“Run! Hurry!”

*Damn it. Too late.*

As if time had been turned backward, the shattered stone gate had been restored.

And the changes didn’t stop there.

The piles of rubble scattered across the ground had formed walls two and three layers deep, while vines from the cavern walls tightly bound them together.

“Everyone, move!”

It was Im Kkeokjeong. His muscles had swollen as though they were about to burst, probably because he’d activated a strength-enhancement Skill. Carrying his tower shield, he charged toward the stone gate.

“Haaah!”

Bang! Crack!

“…Goddamn it.”

Im Kkeokjeong dropped his tower shield, his face blank with disbelief. An E-rank Hunter had used a Skill, yet he hadn’t even broken through. He’d only ruined his shield.

*Enhancement magic?*

Whatever it was, one thing was certain.

That old Hobgoblin, capable of using magic like this, was a monster at least one or two stages above us.

And…

Swish!

There was someone among us who could face it.

*When did he—?*

Team Leader Choi was already charging toward the monster, as if he’d been certain the passage wouldn’t open.

“The wind takes hold. Haste.”

His figure slid forward. The distance of more than a hundred meters vanished in an instant. Five paces from the old Hobgoblin, Team Leader Choi drew the sword from his waist.

Whoosh!

A full-power attack from a C-rank Hunter.

But the old Hobgoblin only gave him an unpleasant smile.

—Karruk. Chwi. Akto.

The change happened in an instant.

Whoosh—

The hundred-odd corpses scattered across the altar shriveled and dispersed like sand. The black energy completely drawn out of them gathered into one mass and slammed into Team Leader Choi’s side.

Thud!

“Ghk.”

Team Leader Choi was knocked back. He landed quickly and spoke with a twisted expression.

“We have to stop it. Right now.”

“That?”

It was already too late.

Only a few seconds ago, it had been nothing more than energy given form. Now, it was rapidly taking shape.

A three-meter-tall giant. A monster with muscles that looked ready to burst and a greatsword of terrifying size.

“What the hell is that…?”

Someone muttered in a dazed voice. Just like me, the others had clearly never seen anything like this before.

Only Team Leader Choi knew what they were.

“Hobgoblin Great Warrior. A C-rank Rare Monster.”

The fact that something so huge was a Hobgoblin was shocking enough. But it was nothing compared to the words that followed.

*C-rank Rare Monster?*

*Fuck, why is that thing showing up here?*

Rare Monsters were monsters that appeared in a given Gate only on rare occasions. And that Great Warrior was C-rank, a being that the rest of us would never have encountered in our entire lives.

…Of course, not anymore.

“Those monsters.”

Team Leader Choi pointed at the old Hobgoblin.

“Ah, fuck…”

“What are our chances? Give it to me straight.”

“If we take out the Priest first, there’s hope. But…”

Boom. Boom.

Team Leader Choi’s words stopped abruptly.

The Great Warrior was approaching us.

One step. Then another.

The cavern floor shook with every step.

*Who?*

“Us?”

“No. You.”

“Kuwooooh!”

The Great Warrior’s roar sent a stalactite dropping from the cavern ceiling.

Team Leader Choi turned around with the most resolute expression I had ever seen on his face.

“The wind takes hold. Haste.”

*Hey, you bastard.*

## Binding rules

# Translation Rules

## Fidelity

- Translate the Korean source—not the wiki, manhwa, fan translations, or expected plot.
- Semantic fidelity outranks elegance. Never improve rhythm, humor, or localization by changing a physical action, negation, relationship, hierarchy, mechanism, quantity, or causal detail.
- Preserve every fact, causal link, joke, emotional beat, repetition, and intentional omission. Add nothing.
- Preserve small action verbs and pragmatic cues exactly: nodding versus shaking one's head, pretending nothing happened, and mild or approachable impressions are characterization, not expendable texture.
- Preserve viewpoint and tense. Resolve omitted subjects only when context supports it; retain genuine ambiguity.
- Match each speaker's hierarchy, intimacy, humor, and profanity naturally. Do not mechanically retain every honorific or classical self-reference.
- Do not censor or soften content.

## Terminology

- `compendium.md` and `docs/NAMES.md` are binding for established names, titles, ranks, techniques, organizations, system terms, items, and locations. Profile headings and aliases join that ledger.
- Search only exact Korean terms already present in the current chapter; the compendium contains future-sensitive entries.
- Never re-romanize established names or invent grand names for uncertain terms. First use of an unlisted name or title almost always needs a footnote or a mapped ledger term.
- Use `qi` for Murim energy and `mana` for the modern Hunter system when the source distinguishes them.
- In System panels, render `등급` as `**Grade:**` for quest, item, skill, and martial-art classifications. Reserve `rank` for Hunter classifications or ordinary prose; never replace a System `Grade` field with `Rank`.

## English and Markdown

- Use contemporary US English and natural action-comedy prose; avoid Korean syntax calques and generic cultivation MTL phrasing.
- File: `translations/NNNN.md`; heading: `# Chapter N`.
- Speech: curly double quotes. Direct thoughts: italics without quotes.
- Use em dashes without spaces, the ellipsis character `…`, and `* * *` for source scene breaks.
- Format each actual game System-message panel as one Markdown blockquote window headed `> **System**`. Keep all consecutive notices, fields, and lines inside that same blockquote; separate windows when prose intervenes. Do not enclose System notices or UI terms in square brackets; the `System` heading and framed blockquote identify the panel. Do not label manuals, ordinary quotations, warnings printed in a manual, or other non-System material as `System`; use a normal blockquote or a specific heading instead. Do not wrap each complete notice in outer `**`; retain bold only for meaningful labels or emphasis inside the panel.
- Keep the final file English-only reading copy: no audit notes, Korean text, summaries, or model metadata.

### Tone and Style

- Write like a polished commercial webnovel: brisk, vivid, accessible, and easy to read aloud.
- Preserve the series’ contrast between danger and comedy. Let absurdity, bad timing, blunt reactions, and grim situations create dark humor without adding jokes absent from the Korean.
- Jin Taekyung’s narration is conversational, observant, self-mocking, and occasionally profane. It may be irreverent even when the situation is serious.
- Keep deadpan punchlines short and well-timed. Do not explain a joke after delivering it.
- Preserve the source's level of explicitness. A euphemism may remain euphemistic even when its meaning is sexual or crude; do not replace it with more graphic English merely for impact.
- Make dialogue spontaneous and character-specific. Preserve hierarchy and intimacy through word choice, address, rhythm, and restraint—not archaic wuxia English.
- Use strong profanity when the Korean is strong, but neither intensify nor sanitize it. Do not make ordinary lines uniformly vulgar. Profanity should reveal mood or relationship.
- Keep action and injury vivid but clear rather than purple. Do not make violence funny unless the source’s framing does.
- Avoid stiff literalism, translator-added melodrama, dated internet slang, and quippy superhero-style banter.
- On the second pass, correct awkward English collocations and word choices without changing meaning or voice. Prefer ordinary, spoken English over stiff Latinate or ceremonial wording when the scene is brisk or comic: “goose bumps” rather than “gooseflesh,” and “laid into them” rather than “launched into a solemn denunciation.” Read the prose aloud and replace any phrase that sounds like a formal essay, legal document, or literal dictionary gloss unless the source deliberately calls for that register.

## Footnotes

Use `[^1]` Markdown footnotes when a brief, factual, spoiler-free explanation materially helps an English reader understand:

- a Korean institution, living arrangement, food, holiday, myth, historical reference, or local custom;
- a Korean word, phrase, idiom, wordplay, or culturally specific image that cannot be conveyed fully by the best natural English analogy;
- a deliberately literal rendering whose cultural or linguistic force would otherwise be lost.

For example, render `고시원` as “goshiwon” when the setting or connotations matter, with a concise footnote explaining that it is a very small, inexpensive room-for-rent housing arrangement. Prefer the best natural English analogy in the prose. Use a literal translation plus a concise footnote when the Korean wording itself matters. Define a term at its first meaningful occurrence and do not repeat the note unnecessarily. Footnotes must be rare, useful, and non-spoiling; do not footnote ordinary vocabulary, fully preserved jokes, or uncertainty. Record consequential uncertainty in `docs/STATE.md`.

## Spoilers and Scope

- Safe profiles contain only facts revealed through the latest completed chapter.
- Never read `characters/spoilers/` during drafting. Reviewers may consult one relevant sealed profile only for a specific unresolved continuity issue after the draft is complete.
- Future knowledge may prevent contradiction but may not add early names, pronouns, certainty, motives, or foreshadowing.
- Translate exactly one requested chapter unless the user explicitly requests a batch. Never modify Korean source files under `source/`.
