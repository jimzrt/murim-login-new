I’d spent seven years in this business and experienced every kind of dirty treatment there was. I’d left the time when I could afford to be picky a long time ago.

I had just started walking when—

“Hey, look who it is!”

I turned at the gravelly voice and saw a hairy man grinning broadly at me.

“Taekyung. You’re Jin Taekyung, right?”

“Uncle Kkeokjeong?”

*This guy’s still alive?*

* * *

His surname was Im. I’d forgotten his given name. I thought I’d heard it once when he introduced himself seven years ago, but I couldn’t remember it.

However, he looked enough like a bandit that everyone called him Im Kkeokjeong.

“How’ve you been all this time?”

“Life as an F-rank Hunter is always the same. How about you?”

“Don’t call me that.”

Im Kkeokjeong gave me a generous smile.

“Call me hyung. We’re not even that far apart in age.”

How many years apart were we again? It was hazy.

“Hyung, how old are you?”

“Forty-five.”

“…”

*What’s with that confidence?*

Fortunately, the social survival skills I’d honed over the years came through. I somehow managed to force a smile.

“Right. I’ll just call you hyung.”

“That’s right, little brother. Hwahahaha!”

His hearty laughter echoed through the auditorium. Nearly a hundred people looked our way, then quickly looked away.

*I should’ve just pretended I didn’t hear him and kept walking.*

In some ways, ours was a shallow connection. We’d seen each other every day for about half a year at the Ilsan office, and occasionally worked together. That was about it.

*He’s a good guy, but…*

Sometimes having him next to me was embarrassing.

Like now.

“The yulmu tea here is incredible. And the auditorium chairs are nice and soft.”

Im Kkeokjeong tossed back a mouthful of yulmu tea[^1] and leaned his chair as far back as it would go.

Judging by the way he handled it, this clearly wasn’t his first or second visit.

“You come here often?”

“Not every day. I drop by now and then. Once I got married and had kids, I started taking fewer risks. Heh heh.”

It seemed he’d started a family while we were out of touch. When I congratulated him, Im Kkeokjeong scratched his head.

“It’s nothing special.”

He was being modest, but it was a big deal. He’d worked as an F-rank Hunter for more than twenty years and even managed to build a family.

For a moment, I wondered if the Im Kkeokjeong in front of me might be a glimpse of my future.

*Of course, I’d have to survive to that age first.*

Being a Hunter wasn’t a career you could keep up for long. Plenty of people retired the moment they completed the ten years required to qualify for a pension.

“Anyway, you’ve started to look the part. When I first met you, you were completely frozen and could barely talk.”

“Of course. I’ve got seven years under my belt.”

“Then have you just been going around to offices ever since? Didn’t you sign with a small or medium-sized Guild on fairly decent terms? So, so… What was the name again?”

“Sopung Guild. They fired me the day before yesterday.”

Im Kkeokjeong forced a laugh.

“Hahaha! Good for you. The Guild’s name was lousy, too. What the hell is Sopung? It’s not like you’re going on a picnic to a Gate.”

“No, Sopung is a place name. It was near Bucheon Sopung Terminal, so they called it Sopung Guild.”

“Oh…”

The conversation that followed was surprisingly useful. Im Kkeokjeong was a regular at the manpower office, after all, and a veteran Hunter with plenty of useful information and his own hard-earned know-how.

“The office takes a ten percent commission. That’s standard. But the hit rate here is pretty good.”

*Hit rate* was slang in this business for the probability of getting hired. Since my immediate goal was to earn a day’s pay, that was good news.

“Even for F-rank Hunters like us?”

“Huh? Yeah. Of course.”

*What was with that awkward expression?*

Before I could ask anything else, a voice came through the speakers installed in the auditorium.

—E-rank Hunter Im Hyeokjun. Im Hyeokjun, please come to the lobby.

Six thirty in the morning.

At last, the first one was called. It would be my turn only after all the E-rank Hunters had left.

“As expected, the E-ranks go first… Hyung, where are you going?”

“I’ll go ahead.”

Im Kkeokjeong—or rather, Im Hyeokjun—slung a large bag containing armor and weapons over his shoulder and gave a sheepish laugh.

*So that was why his expression looked strange.*

It seemed the man had worked himself to the bone while I wasn’t looking.

It might have been only one step, but moving up from F-rank with an F-rank Hunter’s potential was no easy feat.

“See you again.”

“Yes. See you.”

His departure was the beginning. The speakers started pouring out people’s names as if they’d been waiting for this moment.

E-rank this person, E-rank that person, E-rank…

*Damn it, am I the only F-rank here?*

Just as I was starting to get anxious—

—F-rank Hunter Jin Taekyung. Jin Taekyung, please come to the lobby.

*There it is!*

* * *

“Mr. Jin Taekyung?”

A man in a white linen shirt was waiting in the lobby. Without any preamble, he handed me a contract with an impassive expression.

“I’m from Peace Guild. Read it and sign.”

*What an irritating way to talk.*

I glared back and forth between the contract and the man’s face.

“The settlement ratio is eight to two.”

“After the proceeds are fairly divided according to contribution after the raid, we take twenty percent of the amount you receive.”

“What about the base pay?”

“Thirty.”

“Thir…ty?”

*Had this guy’s tongue been cut in half?*

“Gate grade.”

“E-rank.”

“No.”

“The combat positions are full. You’ll be a porter.”

The corner of my eye twitched.

*What the hell did this guy just say?*

“Pooorter?”

“Is there a problem?”

“Obviously.”

The man in the shirt glared at me condescendingly.

“What is it?”

“I don’t have a pen.”

“…”

After a brief silence, I accepted the pen he handed me and scrawled my signature across the contract. The base pay was 300,000 won, and the settlement ratio was generous, too.

I’d flinched when I heard it was an E-rank Gate, but I was only going as a porter, so it didn’t matter. I’d strip a few monster hides, carry a backpack for a while, and part ways in a good mood.

*Peace Guild. I like the name already.*

I’d been itching to sign for so long that my fingers had nearly cramped.

“There. All signed.”

“…”

“So where are we going now? Are we taking a van?”

“There’s a minibus waiting outside.”

“Oh, that one? Looks nice. I bet the air conditioning is powerful, too.”

“…”

“Guess I was the last one? There are already a few people here… Oh? Kkeokjeong hyung!”

“Huh? Taekyung!”

Im Kkeokjeong was waiting to load his luggage into the bus’s trunk. He grinned broadly.

“You’re coming, too. Great!”

“Looks like it. Maybe we really are connected, hyung.”

“Hahahaha!”

“Ahahaha!”

“…Let’s get going.”

Maybe it was the heat, but the man in the shirt suddenly looked much older.

* * *

The bus set off. The man in the passenger seat told us we’d arrive within twenty minutes, then closed his eyes.

Im Kkeokjeong introduced me to the others who had been hired with us.

“All right, everyone, say hello. This is a younger brother of mine.”

Now that I looked around, they all seemed to know one another. I gave a polite bow.

“Hello. I’m Jin Taekyung.”

“Oh, nice to meet you.”

“Young man, you’re quite a looker. You seem like you can fight.”

There were three new people in total, all men in their late thirties or early forties. In the friendly atmosphere, Im Kkeokjeong explained,

“These guys are all E-rank. I’ve known them for a long time. More than ten years, in fact.”

“Yeah, about that long. Time sure flies.”

So they’d all been in the business for at least ten years. I had enough experience that nobody could really call me a novice, but these guys were on another level entirely.

*A party of old-timers.*

People like them pulled their weight wherever they went. In an emergency, they were much more reliable than mediocre D-rank Hunters.

“But what rank is the young man?”

The question I’d been waiting for had arrived.

The rank check.

I answered cautiously.

“F-rank.”

“Oh. Is that right? How many years have you been at it?”

“Seven years.”

“Hmm. Is that right?”

The atmosphere turned lukewarm. That would be a problem if my first raid started like this.

I quickly filled the silence.

“I won’t be taking part in combat at all. I’m joining as a porter, so you don’t have to worry…”

The three men stared blankly at me.

“Why are you explaining all that? It’s not like we’re going to eat you.”

“Forget it. If you’ve been around for seven years, you know enough.”

“If Kkeokjeong hyung recommended him, that’s good enough. Team Leader Choi must’ve thought he was all right, too, or he wouldn’t have said yes.”

*Recommended? Team Leader Choi?*

I didn’t know the details, but I could more or less see how things had played out.

Im Kkeokjeong had recommended me to the man in the shirt—Team Leader Choi.

“W-what are you talking about?”

Im Kkeokjeong waved his hands, his face bright red.

The three men snickered.

“Everyone can tell. How did that hyung ever manage to get married?”

“Good deeds are supposed to come to light. Why hide them so much?”

“Exactly. You agree too, don’t you?”

I promptly nodded.

“Of course. Thank you for looking out for me.”

“Ahem. Well, I just floated your name once. Team Leader Choi made the decision.”

*Well, damn.*

I’d thought of Im Kkeokjeong as one of the many people I’d met briefly before moving on. But after meeting him again, I realized he was more guileless and warmhearted than I’d expected.

*So that’s why the terms were so generous.*

The way they kept calling him Team Leader Choi made me think their friendship had played a part in my contract as well.

“I’ll work hard.”

“I told you, Team Leader Choi made the decision!”

The man in question answered Im Kkeokjeong’s awkward defense.

“Then let’s say I made the decision.”

The man in the shirt had opened his eyes at some point. No, he was Team Leader Choi. He spoke to us.

“We’ve arrived.”

I turned my head and saw a four-meter-high Gate drawing closer. At its center, a vortex of mana churned, ready to suck us in.

*An E-rank Gate.*

This was my first raid since returning.

[^1]: Yulmu tea is a sweet Korean grain drink, commonly served hot or cold.
