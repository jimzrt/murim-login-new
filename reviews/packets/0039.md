A dark, cramped room. A small TV on the desk. A single bed. And one person sleeping there, reeking of alcohol.

“Khrrr. Khrrrrooo.”

Who would have thought those bizarre snores could sound so welcome?

*I’m back. Back to reality.*

Everything was exactly as I remembered it. The memories of the past thirty days felt like a dream.

After staring blankly around the room for a while, I walked over to Jinho hyung. Then I slammed the edge of my hand into his uvula.

Whack!

“Khrrr… gack!”

If it hadn’t been for this man’s snoring, I never would have crawled into that capsule. I grabbed Jinho hyung as he thrashed around.

“Let’s take one more hit. No, just two.”

Whack! Whack!

“Gack! Grrrgh!”

* * *

“You people should know better. And you’re supposed to be the goshiwon manager, no less.”

“…Sorry.”

“Let’s not make this something to be embarrassed about, okay?”

Bang.

Once the door closed, Jinho hyung turned around with a sigh.

“Are you insane?”

Before he could say anything worse, I got ahead of him.

“For the record, it was self-defense.”

“What the fuck are you talking about? Have you lost your mind?”

“I’m perfectly normal.”

“Then why the hell did you hit a sleeping person in the throat and—!”

Thump, thump, thump.

The man next door pounded on the wall. Judging by the rhythm and force, the message was clear: *I want to kill you.*

Jinho hyung lowered his voice.

“Why the hell are you making a racket? At this hour, too.”

“At this hour?”

“Yeah, you lunatic. It’s only three in the morning.”

With an incredulous look on his face, Jinho hyung held out his phone.

July 25. 3:02 a.m.

I checked the date and time, then let my mouth fall open.

*Only three hours have passed?*

I’d spent a whole month in Murim. Exactly thirty days. And in reality, only three hours had gone by.

“…Hyung.”

“Don’t talk to me. My throat hurts.”

“What’s the usual time ratio for a game capsule?”

“You’re like Hong Gil-dong, you bastard. Your conversation keeps popping up in the east, then the west. It’s all over the place.”

“How much is it?”

“Huh?”

When I asked with a stiff face, Jinho hyung looked briefly flustered before answering.

“The latest capsule that came out last month? Five to one, I think.”

“What exactly does five to one mean?”

“What do you think it means? If five hours pass in the game, one hour passes in reality.”

This was driving me crazy.

“What about anything beyond that?”

“There isn’t anything. With current technology, that’s the limit… Why are you asking about this?”

Because something impossible had happened.

*How am I supposed to make sense of this?*

Even after looking at it again, the capsule was ancient. That piece of junk, made more than twenty years ago, had technology far beyond anything available today.

Thirty days in only three hours. What kind of time ratio did that make it? My mind was too scrambled to calculate.

“This is fucking wild.”

“What is?”

It was impossible for me to understand on my own. Maybe Jinho hyung could come up with some kind of answer. After hesitating for a moment, I spoke.

“A month ago—no, three hours ago—I went into that capsule…”

I’d been through so much that I had plenty to say. Even after hearing the whole story, Jinho hyung remained silent for a long time.

Then he said one thing.

“This is fucking wild.”

“Exactly. Does this make any sense? Is that some illegally modified capsule or something?”

“No. Not the capsule. You.”

“Huh?”

Jinho hyung spoke with a serious expression.

“Just apologize properly. Say you’re sorry. Say you hit me because my snoring was too loud. Be cool about it, you bastard.”

“…”

“What, you couldn’t log out and spent a month risking your life in a game? Go write a novel.”

*Right. I knew things were going suspiciously well.*

I sighed.

“I’m telling you, it’s true.”

“Come on, Taekyung. Let’s think rationally.”

Jinho hyung adopted a solemn tone.

“Some guy smacks a sleeping person in the uvula to wake him up, then claims he was trapped in a game for a month. But when he checks the clock, only three hours have passed? And the capsule is an antique that belongs in a museum?”

“I know it sounds insane. I understand. But…”

“Then try to understand me, too. My head hurts from my hangover, my throat hurts, and my heart hurts every time I look at you.”

“Just listen to me!”

Thump, thump, thump, thump.

Before the man next door could break through the wall, I lowered my voice.

“Then try it yourself.”

“What?”

“If you try it, you’ll believe me.”

There was nothing faster than experiencing something firsthand. If I pulled him out after about ten minutes, he’d have no choice but to believe me.

Jinho hyung stared at me for a long time before speaking.

“Fine. Let’s try it, then.”

He climbed into the capsule and put on the VR helmet. As he complained that the inside of the helmet smelled stale, I said,

“I’ll pull you out soon. Stay holed up in Honghwaru.”

“Yeah, right. Just close the capsule door.”

I was curious what expression he’d have when we met again. I closed the capsule door, took out my phone, and turned on the stopwatch.

The instant I pressed the start button, the numbers began climbing rapidly.

*One second, two seconds… ten seconds.*

Ten seconds in reality had to mean minutes, maybe hours, in the game.

*By now, he must be running around Honghwaru trying to figure out what’s going on…*

Clunk.

“Huh?”

The door opened.

*What? Why is he already out? No, how did he get out?*

Jinho hyung sighed as he looked at me in confusion.

“What are you doing?”

“Huh? What?”

“The power isn’t even on. How could you be playing a game?”

What was he talking about?

“The power isn’t on?”

“Move.”

Before I could do anything but stammer, Jinho hyung opened the door and stepped out. He bent down beneath the capsule and picked something up.

“What do you think this is?”

It was a cord. An unplugged power cord.

*Did I see something that wasn’t there?*

I rubbed my eyes furiously, but nothing changed.

“Why is this…”

“Taekyung. Jin Taekyung. You poor, pathetic soul.”

Jinho hyung spoke with a distant look on his face.

“Go to a psychiatric hospital as soon as it gets light. I’m going back to my room.”

He tossed the power cord aside and walked away. I stared blankly at his back.

The power cord hadn’t even been plugged in.

*Then what was the game I played?*

I felt as if I’d been bewitched by a ghost. Goose bumps rose all over my body.

* * *

I lay on the bed and thought.

*Am I crazy?*

I’d played a game for thirty days in a capsule that hadn’t even been plugged in.

I could understand Jinho hyung’s reaction. But everything that had happened there…

*It was all real.*

Jin Wikyung, Wolhwa, Hyuk Mujin, and the Head Elder. I remembered every one of the NPCs’ faces, their ways of speaking, and their actions. It hadn’t been a delusion created by me alone.

*Then what was the problem?*

There was only one answer.

The problem was that ancient capsule—a piece of junk made twenty-seven years ago that should have been retired and sitting in a museum.

Its date of manufacture was suspicious, too.

January 1, 2020.

The day the Demon King Asmodeus fell to humanity.

*Where the hell were people making capsules back then?*

Hundreds of millions of people had died during the five-year Great War, and monsters had roamed the city centers.

In my opinion, anyone who had fired up a factory after hearing the breaking news that the Demon King had fallen and announced, *All right, let’s make game capsules now!* should have been dragged before a judge.

*Back then, I thought it was a misprint.*

I suddenly froze.

*A misprint?*

*The product manual!*

How could I have forgotten something that important? What an idiot.

I shot to my feet and began turning the room upside down. At last, I found a familiar little booklet beneath the bed.

> **Product User Manual**
>
> **Product name:** Virtual Reality Interface  
> **Model name:** Ark - 2020  
> **Manufacturer:** H Soft  
> **Date of manufacture:** January 1, 2020

And the next page.

> **Precautions**
>
> - The player cannot log out at will.
>
> - If the player dies during play, resurrection is impossible.

A month ago—no, three hours ago—I had thrown the booklet aside right there.

But this time was different.

With trembling hands, I turned to the last page.

> **Key Features**
>
> - **A custom capsule for one person only!** Once a user is registered, the capsule is permanently bound to them. This remains in effect until their death.
>
> - **Time-ratio adjustment for comfortable play!** Upon Login, time in reality passes very slowly. The opposite is also true.
>
> - **Character Synchronization system!** By Synchronizing with their character, the user experiences a greater sense of unity.

“What the hell is this?”

My eyes read the words, but my brain refused to process them. I started reading from the beginning again.

*First, permanent binding.*

It meant exactly what it said: the capsule was mine until I died. Once I got some answers, I decided, I was going to smash that damn thing to pieces.

*Next, the time ratio.*

That was the part I’d been most curious about. But instead of an exact figure, all it said was the vague phrase *time passes very slowly*.

Since it said the reverse was true as well, time in the game must be passing too, albeit very slowly.

*Then was the war still going on?*

I suddenly thought of Jin Wikyung. The Head Elder’s betrayal was already a certainty, and he would try to stab Wikyung in the back at the most important moment. Had Hyuk Mujin and the reconnaissance squad told Wikyung about it?

*Ah, then it must still be a long way off. The time ratio had flipped around.*

More importantly, I had a much more immediate problem to deal with. I looked at the last key feature. Unlike the first two, I couldn’t understand this one no matter how many times I read it.

*Synchronize with a character?*

Just in case, I even looked it up in a Korean dictionary. It meant exactly what I thought it meant.

That only made it sound crazier.

*How would you even synchronize with a game character?*

I couldn’t make any sense of it. In the end, all I’d done was add another question to the list.

One thing was certain: I wasn’t crazy.

I collapsed onto the bed and read the front of the booklet again.

“Manufacturer. H Soft.”

In the end, there was only one road to follow. If I investigated these people, I had to find something.

I searched the internet for H Soft, but aside from discovering that there was a pornographic production company with the same name, I couldn’t find out anything.

“…”

First, I needed to lock the door and think.

## Fidelity

- Translate the Korean source—not the wiki, manhwa, fan translations, or expected plot.
- Semantic fidelity outranks elegance. Never improve rhythm, humor, or localization by changing a physical action, negation, relationship, hierarchy, mechanism, quantity, or causal detail.

- Preserve viewpoint and tense. Resolve omitted subjects only when context supports it; retain genuine ambiguity.
- Match each speaker's hierarchy, intimacy, humor, and profanity naturally. Do not mechanically retain every honorific or classical self-reference.
- Do not censor or soften content.

- `compendium.md` and `docs/NAMES.md` are binding for established names, titles, ranks, techniques, organizations, system terms, items, and locations. Profile headings and aliases join that ledger.

- File: `translations/NNNN.md`; heading: `# Chapter N`.

- a deliberately literal rendering whose cultural or linguistic force would otherwise be lost.
