# Chapter 10

After walking for more than ten minutes, we arrived at a cliff blocking off the rear of the Jin Family of Taiyuan estate. A massive cavern yawned open in its face. One person stood before it.

“Hmm. You’ve come?”

A bear of a man with a stern manner of speaking. Jin Wikyung.

I gave him an awkward bow.

“Hello, big… brother.”

Jin Wikyung made me uncomfortable. As if suddenly gaining an NPC family wasn’t enough, he took an extraordinary amount of interest in me.

Just look at him. He was trying his damnedest not to show it in front of everyone else. But if you looked closely, his eyes were moist.

*Is this guy seriously that sentimental?*

Outwardly, he looked like someone who could make even a triad boss step aside, but I had no idea why all the characters in this game were such bizarre cases.

“I could no longer overlook your conduct, so in my capacity as Lesser Family Head and acting Family Head, I ordered you into closed-door training. Do you have anything to say?”

*Of course I do.*

But this was all a staged performance. On the way to the training hall, Wipeng had explained the situation to me. There was a power struggle among the factions within the Jin Family of Taiyuan, and Jin Wikyung was acting as my shield.

So this forced closed-door training was just a show.

> The Young Master did say he needed somewhere to train, didn’t he? Just endure it for three days.

Remembering Wipeng’s final words, I lowered my head and pretended to reflect on my actions.

“I will gladly accept my punishment.”

The best thing about this play was how short the lines were. Jin Wikyung delivered his final line with sorrow in his eyes.

“Lock the criminal in the training hall. The date of his release will be announced later.”

The moment he finished speaking, the two warriors guarding the entrance approached and seized me by both arms.

The play was over. Time to exit the stage.

I stood at the entrance to the training hall.

> I’ve had everything you need brought inside. Youngest, don’t push yourself too hard.

With Jin Wikyung’s Sound Transmission in my ears, I took my first step inside.

* * *

The training hall was, in a word, a cave. An artificial cave carved into the cliff, to be precise.

It was high, spacious, and damp.

Squelch, squelch.

How long had I been following the warrior assigned to the training hall? Just as I began wondering whether I was wearing leather shoes or a pair of floor mops, a massive iron gate appeared before me.

*Whoa…*

My mouth fell open the moment I saw it. Filling the passageway without a gap, it looked less like a gate and more like a wall barring all passage in or out.

The warrior guiding me raised his torch and shouted,

“Open the gate!”

Groooan—

The enormous iron gate slowly opened its maw. It wasn’t some magical “open sesame” spell. An NPC who had been waiting nearby had simply pulled on the protruding gate mechanism.

Even so, it was an overwhelming sight.

And the moment I saw what lay beyond it—

“Wow.”

This time, even I couldn’t suppress the exclamation that escaped me.

When I first entered the training hall, I had expected a damp underground cave. But…

“What is all this?”

There was a spacious bed covered with a furry blanket that made me feel better just looking at it. Instead of a damp, uneven stone floor, a clean gray surface stretched out before me.

*It obviously isn’t cement. Limestone, maybe?*

Aside from the chill in the air—or even taking that into account—the environment was far better than I could have imagined.

*This is supposed to be punishment?*

I was still staring around in a daze when someone cleared his throat behind me. I turned to find the warrior who had guided me there.

“We have prepared everything you require. Then I shall take my leave.”

Groooan.

As I watched the iron gate slowly close, I suddenly remembered the Sound Transmission I had heard at the entrance.

> I’ve had everything you need brought inside. Youngest, don’t push yourself too hard.

Ah. The heart of an NPC who cared about his little brother.

This pathetic user weeps his heart out.

* * *

It happened all the time in the real world, too. Powerful people who had committed corruption entering the prosecutor’s office in wheelchairs, or checking themselves into private hospital suites to evade an investigation.

I was in much the same situation now, though not quite identical. With a moved expression, I looked around the private suite in the training hall.

“So this is what life is like when you’re born with a gold spoon.”[^1]

The graphics and artificial intelligence weren’t the only realistic things about this game. It even carried a social message: no matter how hard you struggled, nothing beat being born rich.

Good grief. What would I have done if I hadn’t gotten this character? His father was the Family Head, his eldest brother was the Lesser Family Head, and his second brother was a martial arts genius. No wonder he could spend all his time haunting pleasure houses without a care in the world.

*Jin Taekyung, you bastard…*

As it turned out, wasn’t he a great sage who had grasped how the world worked at a tender age?

Shivering at the realistic gold-spoon system, I wandered around the training hall, which covered more than three hundred square meters. Eventually, I found the “essentials” the warrior had mentioned.

*Food first.*

The food had been divided between two jars. When I looked inside, I found rice balls reeking strongly of medicinal herbs.

> **System**
>
> Item Window
>
> **Excellent Grain-Avoidance Pills**
>
> **Type:** Pellet
>
> **Grade:** First Rate
>
> **Restriction:** None
>
> **Description:** Grain-avoidance pills made by cramming all kinds of beneficial medicinal ingredients into them with brute force. Restores vitality when consumed and grants additional effects when taken over a long period.

“Oh, so this is the famous grain-avoidance pill?”

I had seen them countless times in martial arts novels. They were light, compact, easy to carry, and apparently provided nutritional support as well.

The other jar was full of them, too.

*I should put these in my inventory for now.*

I gripped the jars with both hands and muttered,

“Acquire Item.”

If anyone else had seen me, they would have fallen over in shock. The two jars that had been sitting there perfectly normally vanished as if they had evaporated.

I gazed fondly at the jars stored in my inventory.

*That takes care of the food problem.*

The second essential item I found was water. The inside of the training hall was, after all, a cave, and a cold underground spring in one corner solved the problem of drinking water.

And then there was the third and final item.

“Hmm.”

A weapons rack held several weapons in a neat row. Naturally, the first one I picked up was a sturdy-looking wooden spear.

*Item Appraisal.*

Ding.

> **System**
>
> Item Window
>
> **Training Wooden Spear**
>
> **Type:** Weapon
>
> **Grade:** Third Rate
>
> **Restriction:** None
>
> **Description:** Made for beginners.

“Oh.”

A training spear made for beginners. Exactly what I needed right now.

There was only one more thing that would make this perfect.

“Open Inventory.”

Grinning, I pulled out the martial arts manual titled **Jin Family’s Spear Technique**.

Ding.

> **System**
>
> - Would you like to learn **Jin Family’s Spear Technique**? (3 / 10)

“Obviously.”

I really liked the System.

Sometimes, I even found it adorable.

* * *

Yesterday, while learning the Jin Family’s Manoeuvre Technique, I discovered something for the first time.

Not every System notification went *ding*.

I also discovered how much I hated the other sound.

Beep!

> **System**
>
> - The movement has failed.
>
> - Remaining successful attempts (2 / 100)

Whenever I botched a move, the failure message appeared in a System window without fail. I had no idea how many times I had seen it by now.

I looked at the **Training Wooden Spear** in my hand.

*I was wrong.*

Maybe it was because it was made of wood, but the spear was light. It moved quickly whether I thrust it or swung it.

That was the problem.

*It’s too light. I can’t control it properly.*

Fine adjustments were difficult, so I kept slipping up. There was no chance the brutally picky System would overlook even the slightest mistake.

Beep.

> **System**
>
> - The movement has failed.
>
> - Remaining successful attempts (5 / 100)

“Argh. Fuck.”

In the end, I tossed aside the **Training Wooden Spear** and pulled the **Sharp Spear** from my inventory. Its length and the thickness of its shaft were roughly the same as the spear I had used in the real world.

Why hadn’t I taken it out from the beginning?

“Damn, this thing is heavy. Seriously.”

It had been made from solid steel, so its weight was no joke. The monster felt as if it weighed nearly fifty kilograms.

If I swung this for several hours, my stamina wouldn’t hold out.

My current realm was Second Rate. Even with the System’s assistance, swinging more than half a sack of rice around like a pinwheel was too much.

*It’s not like tiger power is going to spring out of nowhere.*

That was when it hit me.

“…Huh?”

What had I just said? Tiger power?

“There is?”

This was a game. It had a System and stats. And it had internal energy.

Ten years of internal energy that I could draw out through a cultivation technique!

It was embarrassing that I had forgotten about it, even for a moment.

“I should have tried using it before…”

They said only someone who had eaten meat knew its taste. There was a reason I was an F-rank Hunter. I had no mana worth a damn and relied entirely on my bare body, so even other Hunters treated me like a useless nobody.

*At least I’ve solved one problem.*

I let out an incredulous chuckle and picked up the spear. Then I slowly and carefully drew up my internal energy.

The formula of the Jin Family’s Cultivation Technique, etched into my mind by the System, replayed rapidly in my head, guiding my internal energy along its prescribed path.

Tingle.

The response was immediate.

The ten years of internal energy curled up in my dantian spread throughout my body. I could feel that power—something I could sense only because this was a game and I was a martial artist—extending through every limb and bone.

*This is…*

Strength overflowed from my entire body. My vastly enhanced physical abilities and senses sent another thrill of ecstasy through this lifelong F-rank Hunter.

*How can things change this much?*

I gripped the spear and began practicing the **Jin Family’s Spear Technique**. The fifty-kilogram steel spear no longer felt heavy. It thrust and slashed through the air, following the paths I wanted it to take.

Eventually—

Ding.

> **System**
>
> - Remaining successful attempts (6 / 100)

The notification I had been waiting for began to ring.

* * *

Jin Wikyung spoke with a worried expression.

“He’s doing well, right?”

“I’m sure he is. If he has any shame.”

“His body hasn’t even fully recovered yet… He’ll be all right, won’t he?”

“If a stranger heard you, they’d think the Third Young Master was on death’s doorstep. At this point, even a little spit would cure him.”

“No. You only say that because you don’t know how frail the youngest has been since childhood.”

Wipeng answered with an incredulous look.

“The elixirs and tonics that have gone into the Third Young Master alone would fill an entire room. And have you already forgotten? The hundred-year snow ginseng theft last year!”

“Now, that was…”

“At the time, the Hall Master of Medicine King Hall went berserk, shouting that he was going to cut open the Third Young Master’s stomach. Honestly, even while trying to stop him, I found myself thinking that it would count as self-defense.”

Jin Wikyung subtly looked away. In the end, the matter had been settled by compensating the Hall Master with money from Jin Wikyung’s personal fortune, but the Hall Master’s fury at the time had been extraordinary.

“He gulped down that many elixirs, so I can’t say for certain, but he probably won’t suffer so much as a minor illness for the rest of his life.”

“Still not enough. Can’t you tell just by looking at him? Every time I see the youngest, I feel sorry for him. He looks like a skeleton with a few scraps of flesh stuck to it. He’s always so sickly and weak in the mornings.”

“Weak?”

Wipeng suddenly remembered a rumor he had heard in the past.

The courtesans of Taiyuan’s red-light district supposedly called Jin Taekyung the Fire King.[^2]

*Just how potent could he be?*

The medicine must have worked exceptionally well. Without realizing it, Wipeng lifted his forearm and tried to imagine the size.

“What are you doing?”

“Ah. Nothing.”

Jin Wikyung sighed as he looked at the mountain of documents piled before him.

“There are countless things to worry about, both with the youngest and inside and outside the family. Especially… the fact that *they* have made contact is troubling.”

“You mean the Mount Heng Sword Sect.”

Mount Heng Sword Sect. The weight of that name was far from light.

Several decades ago, an undefeated wandering martial artist had hung up its signboard. Since then, the sect had grown at a terrifying speed, and now it had reached the point where it threatened the standing of the Jin Family of Taiyuan.

“What could their intentions be?”

“I’ve sent my subordinates to find out.”

Jin Wikyung fidgeted with the letter from the Mount Heng Sword Sect.

Why were they coming? What was their purpose? One question led to another, until he arrived at a single conclusion.

“Notify every branch in Shanxi. Whatever the Mount Heng Sword Sect’s purpose may be, tell them to make every possible preparation.”

This was Murim.

Only those who were prepared would survive and live to see tomorrow.

[^1]: “Gold spoon” is a Korean metaphor for someone born into wealth and privilege.

[^2]: The Korean nickname *Yawang* sounds like “Night King,” but here its first syllable is written with a rare character meaning “flame,” producing the pun “Fire King.”
