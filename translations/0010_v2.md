# Chapter 10

After walking for more than ten minutes, we arrived at the cliff that blocked off the rear of the Jin Family of Taiyuan estate. A cavern yawned wide in its face, and someone stood before it.

“Hmm. You’ve come.”

Built like a brown bear, with a solemn way of speaking. It was Jin Wikyung. I awkwardly dipped my head.

“Hello, hyung…nim.”

Jin Wikyung was an uncomfortable presence. As if suddenly acquiring an NPC family wasn’t enough, he also paid an absurd amount of attention to me.

Just look at him. He was trying his damnedest not to show it with everyone watching, but if you looked closely, his eyes were moist.

*Is this guy seriously that sentimental?*

From the outside, he looked like someone who could make even a Triad boss back down, yet every character in this game was a complete mess in one way or another.

“Your conduct can no longer be overlooked. As the Lesser Family Head and acting Family Head, I have ordered you into closed-door confinement. Do you have anything to say?”

*Of course I do.*

But this was all a staged performance. On the way to the training hall, Wipeng had explained the situation to me. There was a power struggle among the factions within the Jin Family of Taiyuan, and Jin Wikyung was acting as my shield.

In other words, this forced confinement was just for show.

*“Didn’t the Young Master say he needed somewhere to train anyway? Just put up with it for three days.”*

Remembering Wipeng’s final words, I lowered my head and pretended to reflect on my actions.

“I will gladly accept my punishment.”

The best part about this play was that the lines were short. Jin Wikyung delivered his final line with a sorrowful look in his eyes.

“Lock the criminal in the training hall. His release date will be announced later.”

No sooner had he finished speaking than the two warriors guarding the entrance approached and seized me by both arms. The performance was over. Time to exit the stage.

I stood at the entrance to the training hall.

*“I’ve had everything you need brought inside. Little brother, don’t overdo it.”*

With Jin Wikyung’s Sound Transmission in my ears, I took my first step inside.

* * *

The training hall was, in a word, a cave. An artificial cave carved into the cliff, at that. It was tall, spacious, and damp.

Splash. Splash.

How long had I followed the warrior assigned to the training hall? By the time I could no longer tell whether I was wearing leather shoes or wet rags, a massive iron gate appeared before me.

*Wow…*

My mouth fell open the instant I saw it. Filling the passageway without a gap, it looked less like a gate than a wall forbidding all entry.

The warrior who had escorted me raised his torch and shouted,

“Open the gate!”

Grroooan—

The enormous iron gate slowly opened its jaws. It wasn’t some magical “open sesame” spell. An NPC who had been standing by simply pulled the opening mechanism that was sticking out.

Even so, it was an overwhelming sight.

And the moment I saw what lay beyond it—

“Whoa.”

This time, even I couldn’t hide the exclamation that escaped me.

When I first entered the training hall, I’d imagined a damp underground cave, but…

“What is all this?”

There was a spacious bed covered with a fur blanket that lifted my spirits just by looking at it. Instead of a damp, uneven stone floor, a clean gray surface stretched across the room.

*Obviously not cement. Limestone, maybe?*

Aside from the chill in the air—or even taking that into account—the environment was far better than I had imagined.

*This is supposed to be punishment?*

I was looking around in a daze when I heard someone clear his throat behind me. I turned around to see the warrior who had escorted me.

“We have prepared everything you require. I shall take my leave, then.”

As I watched the iron gate slowly close with another groan, I suddenly remembered the Sound Transmission I had heard at the entrance.

*“I’ve had everything you need brought inside. Little brother, don’t overdo it.”*

Ah. The heart of an NPC who cared about his little brother.

This pathetic user wailed at the top of his lungs.

* * *

This sort of thing happened all the time in the real world too. High-ranking officials who had committed corruption would go in and out of the prosecutors’ office in wheelchairs or check themselves into private hospital rooms to avoid investigation.

My situation was slightly different, but I was putting on much the same show. I gazed at the private suite in the training hall with a deeply moved expression.

“So this is what life with a golden spoon is like.”

It wasn’t just the graphics and artificial intelligence that were realistic. The game even carried the social message that no matter how high you flew or how low you crawled, nothing beat being born rich.

Thank goodness I had this character. His father was the Family Head, his eldest brother was the Lesser Family Head, and his second brother was a martial arts genius. No wonder he could spend his life as a fixture at pleasure houses without a care in the world.

*Jin Taekyung, you bastard…*

As it turned out, hadn’t he understood the ways of the world at a young age and become some kind of great sage?

Shivering at this realistic, god-tier golden-spoon system, I walked around the training hall, which covered more than three hundred square meters. Then I found the “essential supplies” the warrior had mentioned.

*Food first.*

The food was divided between two jars. When I peered inside, I saw fist-sized rice balls giving off a strong medicinal smell.

> **System**
>
> Item Window
>
> **Excellent Fasting Pill**
>
> **Type:** Pill
>
> **Grade:** First Rate
>
> **Restriction:** None
>
> **Description:** A fasting pill made by brutally stuffing all kinds of good medicinal ingredients into it. Restores vitality when consumed and grants additional effects when taken regularly over a long period.

“Ah, so this is the famous fasting pill?”

I had seen them plenty of times in martial arts novels. They were light, compact, easy to carry, and apparently provided nutritional support as well. The other jar was packed full of them too.

*I should put these in my inventory for now.*

I gripped the jars with both hands and muttered,

“Item Acquired.”

If someone else had seen me then, they would have fallen over in shock. Two jars that had been sitting there perfectly normally vanished as if they had evaporated.

I gazed fondly at the jars now stored in my inventory.

*That takes care of food.*

The second essential item I discovered was water. The training hall was, after all, a cave, and a cold underground spring in one corner solved the drinking-water problem.

Then came the third and final essential.

“Hmm.”

Several weapons hung neatly from a rack. Naturally, the first thing I picked up was a sturdy-looking wooden spear.

*Item Appraisal.*

Ding.

> **System**
>
> Item Window
>
> **Training Wooden Spear**
>
> **Type:** Weapon
>
> **Grade:** Third Rate
>
> **Restriction:** None
>
> **Description:** Made for beginners.

“Oh.”

A training spear made for beginners. It was exactly what I needed right now. There was only one thing that would make this perfect.

“Open Inventory.”

Grinning, I pulled out the martial arts manual titled **Jin Family’s Spear Technique**.

Ding.

> **System**
>
> - Would you like to acquire **Jin Family’s Spear Technique**? (3 / 10)

“Obviously yes.”

I really liked the System. Sometimes, I even found it adorable.

* * *

Yesterday, while learning the Jin Family’s Manoeuvre Technique, I discovered that System alerts didn’t always go *ding*.

I also discovered just how much I hated the other sound.

Beep!

> **System**
>
> - The movement has failed.
>
> - Remaining successful attempts (2 / 100)

The failure message. The so-called beep appeared without fail every time I botched a movement. I had no idea how many times I had seen it by now.

I looked down at the **Training Wooden Spear** in my hand.

*I was thinking about this all wrong.*

Maybe because it was made of wood, the spear was light. It moved quickly whether I thrust or swung it.

That was the problem.

*It’s too light. I can’t control it properly.*

Because making minute adjustments was difficult, I kept slipping up. The System was such a damn stickler that it had no intention of overlooking mistakes that minor.

Beep.

> **System**
>
> - The movement has failed.
>
> - Remaining successful attempts (5 / 100)

“Ah, fuck.”

In the end, I threw aside the **Training Wooden Spear** and pulled the **Sharp Spear** from my inventory. Its length and the thickness of its shaft were roughly the same as the spear I had used in the real world.

So why hadn’t I taken it out from the start?

“Damn, this thing’s heavy. Seriously.”

Since it was made of solid steel, the weight was no joke. The monster felt as if it weighed nearly fifty kilograms.

If I swung something like this for several hours, my stamina wouldn’t last.

My current realm was Second Rate. Even with the System’s help, swinging more than half a sack of rice like a pinwheel was impossible.

*It’s not as though the strength of a tiger is going to come surging out of nowhere.*

That was when it hit me.

“…Huh?”

What had I just said? The strength of a tiger?

“I do have it.”

This was a game. There was a System and stats. And there was internal energy. Ten years’ worth of internal energy that could be drawn out through a cultivation technique!

It was embarrassing that I had forgotten about it, even for a moment.

“I should have tried using that from the beginning…”

They say you only know the value of meat once you’ve tasted it. Was it any wonder I was an F-rank Hunter? I had no mana to speak of, so I had to make do with my bare body. Even among Hunters, that made me a half-baked joke.

*At least I’ve solved one problem.*

I let out an absurd little laugh and picked up the spear. Then, slowly and carefully, I drew up my internal energy.

The formula of the Jin Family’s Cultivation Technique, engraved into my mind by the System, rapidly played itself back and guided my internal energy along its prescribed path.

A jolt ran through me.

The response was immediate.

The ten years of internal energy curled up in my dantian spread throughout my body. I could feel that energy—something I could experience only because this was a game and I was a martial artist—reaching every limb and bone.

*This is…*

Strength overflowed from every part of my body. My dramatically enhanced physical abilities and senses once again bestowed ecstasy on me, a man who had lived as an F-rank Hunter.

*I can change this much?*

I gripped the spear and began performing the **Jin Family’s Spear Technique**. The fifty-kilogram steel spear no longer felt heavy. It thrust and slashed through the air along the paths I wanted it to take.

Then, at last—

Ding.

> **System**
>
> - Remaining successful attempts (6 / 100)

The notification I had been waiting for began to ring.

* * *

Jin Wikyung spoke with worry written across his face.

“He’s doing well, right?”

“He should be. Assuming he has any shame.”

“He still hasn’t fully recovered… He’ll be all right, won’t he?”

“If someone who didn’t know him saw this, they’d think the Third Young Master was on death’s door. At that point, even a little spit would heal him.”

“No. You only say that because you don’t know how frail my little brother has been since childhood.”

Wipeng answered with an incredulous expression.

“The elixirs and tonics that have gone into the Third Young Master alone would fill an entire room. And have you already forgotten last year’s hundred-year snow ginseng theft?”

“Ahem. That was…”

“The Hall Master of Medicine King Hall was so furious that he ran around threatening to cut open the Third Young Master’s stomach. To be honest, even while stopping him, I found myself thinking it would have been self-defense.”

Jin Wikyung subtly looked away. In the end, the matter had been settled by compensating the Hall Master out of Jin Wikyung’s personal fortune, but the man’s fury at the time had been extraordinary.

“After swallowing that many elixirs, he probably won’t suffer from minor illnesses until the day he dies.”

“Even that isn’t enough. Can’t you tell just by looking at him? Every time I see my little brother, I feel sorry for him. He looks like a skeleton with a few scraps of flesh stuck to it. He’s so feeble every morning that he barely has any strength.”

“He has no strength?”

Wipeng suddenly remembered a rumor he had once heard. Among the courtesans of Taiyuan’s red-light district, Jin Taekyung was supposedly known by the nickname Yawang.[^1]

*Just how much are we talking about?*

*Looks like those medicines really did their job.*

Without realizing it, Wipeng lifted his forearm and tried to imagine the size.

“What are you doing?”

“Ah, nothing.”

Jin Wikyung sighed as he looked at the mountain of paperwork piled up before him.

“There’s my little brother, and no end of things to worry about both inside and outside the family. Especially… the fact that *they* reached out to us is unsettling.”

“You mean the Mount Heng Sword Sect.”

The name Mount Heng Sword Sect carried considerable weight.

Several decades ago, an undefeated wandering martial artist had hung up its signboard. Since then, the sect had grown at a frightening speed, and by now it had become powerful enough to threaten the position of the Jin Family of Taiyuan.

“What could their intentions be?”

“I’ve sent my subordinates to find out.”

Jin Wikyung fidgeted with the letter from the Mount Heng Sword Sect.

Why were they coming? What was their purpose? The questions led endlessly into one another, but in the end, he reached a single conclusion.

“Send word to every branch in Shanxi. Whatever the Mount Heng Sword Sect’s purpose, tell them to make thorough preparations.”

This was Murim.

Only those who were prepared would survive to see tomorrow.

[^1]: A nickname implying a man who reigns over the night.
