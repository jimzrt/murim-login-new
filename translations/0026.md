# Chapter 26

“So…”

Gong Yacheong looked at me with a strange expression.

“So you are that Young Master Jin Taekyung.”

The moment had finally arrived. I let go of the collar of Hyuk Mujin, who had passed out for the third time, and offered an awkward smile.

“Yes. I’m Jin Taekyung.”

The ears of the reconnaissance squad members preparing camp perked up. We were on our way back to the main family.

We had ridden for more than five hours without stopping since leaving the cabin where the battle had taken place. Gong Yacheong, who had kept silent the entire way, had finally spoken for the first time.

*If it weren’t for that bastard.*

I glared at Hyuk Mujin, who was being dragged along by one of the more perceptive squad members. I should have hit him one more time before letting go.

As I regretted my decision, Gong Yacheong spoke.

“I’ve heard plenty about you, Young Master.”

“…I see.”

“Even a three-year-old child knows who you are. You must be the main family’s biggest celebrity.”

At this point, I thought it would be easier to find someone who didn’t know me.

“You’re lazy by nature and arrogant beyond belief. You’re lost in wine and women and don’t even practice martial arts… But I see the rumors of the martial world aren’t worth believing after all. The martial prowess you displayed today was truly remarkable.”

“…”

If there were some sort of Martial World Daily that collected all the rumors of the martial world, I’d want to subscribe immediately. It was surprisingly credible.

*More importantly…*

Did Gong Yacheong really not know?

Whatever kind of bullshit the Mount Heng Sword Sect had pulled, I was at the center of this entire mess.

It was an undeniable fact that countless people had died along the way.

It wouldn’t have been strange if Gong Yacheong and Socheon held a grudge against me. That was why I had hidden my name.

*If he really doesn’t know, that’s a relief.*

We were being chased by a ruthless Peak master. I had no interest in causing unnecessary trouble.

“How long until we reach the main family?”

Gong Yacheong’s question snapped me back to reality.

“We should arrive in two or three days.”

“Of all times, it had to snow now…”

Gong Yacheong lamented as he glared resentfully at the sky, which was still pouring down snow.

“Young Master, we should prepare for the worst.”

“The worst?”

The blizzard had trapped us, but it had trapped our enemies, too. There was even a chance they had abandoned the pursuit.

Gong Yacheong shook his head.

“Don’t underestimate wandering martial artists. The purity of their internal energy and the level of the martial arts they’ve learned may be lacking, but their greatest weapon is something else.”

I gave him the answer.

“Experience.”

“Exactly. The experience gained from fighting a hundred battles. The ones chasing us aren’t ordinary martial artists. They’re wandering martial artists. They may be similar, but they’re entirely different creatures.”

Similar, but different. I thought I understood what he meant.

In short, they were well-trained hunting dogs. And the man holding their leash was Jopil, One Question, One Kill.

“The reason Jopil is truly frightening isn’t simply because he’s a Peak master. It’s his persistence. His viciousness. He has never once failed a commission or allowed a target to escape.”

I answered with a sigh.

“It’ll be the same this time, then.”

“Probably.”

Gong Yacheong stared into the darkness beyond us, as though something might leap out and swallow us whole.

“We’re being chased by a man like that.”

Whoosh. The snow-filled wind melted into the darkness.

* * *

We finished preparing camp. We had dug several small burrows and blocked their entrances with branches, fallen leaves, and whatever else we could find. It was crude, but this was no time to be choosy.

“Have a comfortable night.”

Gong Yacheong had already disappeared, carrying the sleeping siblings in his arms. The exhausted smile hanging from the corners of his mouth kept coming back to me.

*He really is an adult.*

If he had wanted to live, he could have run away long ago. Gong Yacheong was a skilled martial artist and an experienced man of the martial world.

But he hadn’t.

He had risked his life fighting for two children who didn’t share a drop of his blood.

That was why Gong Yacheong was a good adult.

*Could I have done the same?*

I brushed the question aside as soon as it arose. This was a game. There was no point thinking too deeply about an illusion that would soon disappear.

That was right. An illusion that would soon disappear.

*Open Quest Window.*

Ding.

> **System**
>
> **Logout**
>
> You must now make your way through this harsh Murim.
>
> Become stronger. Become famous.
>
> For the day that will come someday…
>
> **Grade:** Main Quest
>
> **Limit:** Jin Taekyung
>
> **Task:** Reach the First Rate realm — Incomplete  
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Reach Lv. 30 — 24/30  
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Reach Fame 500 — 250/500
>
> **Reward:** **Logout**

The EXP and Fame I had received for dealing with the enemies. The Quest reward on top of that.

I could finally see the summit of this treacherous mountain.

I opened my Status Window and distributed all my remaining points. Vitality flowed back into my exhausted body.

*I’m almost there.*

I only had to get past this hurdle. If I could avoid that bastard Jopil, One Question, One Kill—the man whose face I didn’t even know—I could finally put an end to this miserable journey.

But there was another problem.

*Let’s say I can somehow fill up my Level and Fame. How am I supposed to raise my realm?*

Back when I first started clearing the tutorial Quests, I had raised my realm from Third Rate to Second Rate by distributing my stats. But after that, nothing had happened.

*What the hell am I missing?*

I was an abnormal existence in Murim. I could learn martial arts and raise my stats faster than anyone else. I had grown stronger with the System’s help in all kinds of ways.

That was why I could literally wipe the floor with ordinary First Rate martial artists despite being a common Second Rate.

And yet I wasn’t First Rate!

*I should’ve asked Jin Wikyung.*

At first, I thought I just needed to keep raising my stats or internal energy. But lately, I had started to feel that I was looking in completely the wrong place.

*Level? Stats? Internal energy?*

What was it? The final piece needed to complete this puzzle?

The answer came quickly.

*If I don’t know, I have to find out.*

Just like I had until now. If I kept walking, I would find an exit.

I sat cross-legged and regulated my breathing. The internal energy coiled in my dantian responded to my call.

* * *

“We lost them.”

Jopil muttered as his black, viper-like eyes swept over the surroundings.

They had lost the fugitives. But traces remained. The faint traces they had left behind would become new signposts.

“About twenty people. They left this place roughly two shichen ago.”[^1]

Jopil nodded at his subordinate’s report. The man’s martial arts were only Third Rate, but his tracking skills were Peak. Jopil had countless hunting dogs like that under his command.

*Two shichen.*

The distance had been reduced to two shichen. How long would it take to catch the fugitives?

Half a day at most.

*They should have run with everything they had. Without looking back. They should have thrown off every burden, abandoned any stragglers, and kept running for their lives.*

If they had done that, the distance would not have narrowed. But they had let their guard down. They must have thought the blizzard had trapped them.

That difference had created the current situation.

*But I’m a little different.*

The previous night had been long. Even among his subordinates, men who had become hardened to pursuit, there had been stragglers.

In the mountains during a blizzard, becoming separated from the group was synonymous with death.

The only difference from dying on a battlefield was that it took a little longer.

That was why Jopil had given the order.

*Kill them.*

People said that when a tiger died, it left its hide behind, while a person left behind a name.

But Jopil thought differently. What kind of name could a Third Rate nobody who had spent his entire life drifting through back alleys leave behind?

They should at least leave a hide.

Leaving twenty stripped corpses behind, they continued onward. After the long night ended, they found the fugitives’ traces just before dawn.

*We’ll be meeting soon, friend.*

The corners of Jopil’s mouth lifted. A mysterious master—the first in a long while to catch his interest.

His stiffened heart began to thump excitedly.

* * *

I shivered.

“Ugh. What the hell?”

A chill had suddenly run through me. When I raised my arm, goose bumps had risen all over it.

If this were a scene in a novel, the protagonist would have muttered, *Something feels off,* and continued along his path.

But I was different. I had a solid hunch.

“Hey. Get over here, quick.”

I couldn’t see him, but I could feel it. The footsteps of someone trudging along behind me faltered.

“W-why?”

“I’m counting to three. One, two, three.”

The moment I finished counting, Hyuk Mujin hurried over and pressed himself against my side.

“It was you, wasn’t it?”

“What?”

“You were the one. Tell me the truth and I’ll let it slide.”

“What? I didn’t do anything!”

I silently stared at his face, swollen like a steamed bun.

“You were cursing me behind my back just now, weren’t you?”

“Hah.”

“You were cursing me, right?”

“I-I was…”

So it really had been this bastard. When I raised my hand, Hyuk Mujin squeezed his eyes shut.

After getting beaten about three times, he had lost all his fighting spirit. On top of that, he had learned a valuable life lesson:

Dodging only meant getting hit more.

“Fine. Since you told the truth, I’ll let it go this once.”

Hyuk Mujin jerked his head up.

“Really?”

I gave him a warm smile.

“Of course. But don’t even think about deceiving me from now on. I’ll be watching you with mind-reading.”

Hyuk Mujin looked at me with eyes full of demons, then bolted back to his place. If I’d had enough time, I would have crushed his head with a mace.

Swallowing my regret, I continued walking.

“What’s mind-reading?”

The chattering voice made my ears itch. It belonged to Soyul, Socheon’s little sister. Was she five years old?

She was small enough to fit right inside my backpack.

“Is it martial arts?”

“Something like that.”

“Is mind-reading strong?”

“Very.”

“Wow! Soyul wants to learn mind-reading, too!”

“But you have to be one-eyed.”

“Gasp!”

I turned my head slightly and saw Soyul staring at me in shock. Her wide, round eyes were unbelievably cute.

*Haeyeon used to be like that, too.*

These days she was a creepy little sister, but when she was young she had been a baby angel. She had even received offers to become a child model…

Carrying Soyul around felt exactly like carrying Haeyeon around at that age.

“Do you want me to teach you?”

“…Soyul doesn’t like martial arts. I want to become a proper young lady.”

“That’s fine, too.”

“Um. Mister, do you like martial arts?”

“Me?”

“My brother says you’re really strong. Dad said only people who work hard can become strong.”

“Your dad said that?”

“Yes. My dad is really strong, too. Because…”

She chattered excitedly for a while before suddenly sticking out her lower lip.

“Soyul wants to see Dad. But I guess Dad doesn’t want to see us. Oppa says he went out to play with Mom, leaving me and Oppa behind.”

My heart dropped.

An old memory clouded my vision. In a funeral hall filled with black and white, little Haeyeon had searched for our father, and I had no choice but to tell her an obvious lie.

Just as Socheon had done for Soyul.

It was the only thing I could say.

“…I see.”

What else could I say?

I looked at Socheon, who was following along in the middle of the formation. He was breathing heavily and soaked in sweat.

*He must be exhausted.*

His willpower was far beyond his years. The saying that pain made people mature was fucking bullshit, but it was true.

Socheon had never fallen behind even once, and the other reconnaissance squad members admired him for it.

*But the real problem is somewhere else.*

Gong Yacheong.

His face was pale, and he still hadn’t recovered from his injuries. If not for the remarkable effects of the fasting pills I had given him and the qi he had circulated last night, he might have collapsed long ago.

*At this rate, we’ll be caught.*

Even after the sun rose, the snow that had accumulated on the ground did not melt easily. I was leading the way and clearing a path through it, but everyone was exhausted, and our pace had slowed.

On top of that, we had an injured man and two children.

*Should I run away by myself?*

The thought came to me in an instant.

It was a game. So what?

Gong Yacheong, the two children, and all the reconnaissance squad members were artificial intelligences created by the game. They were nothing more than NPCs.

But what about me?

I was alive. I was the only one among them who was real—a physical being.

But…

*Fuck. It’s a simple problem… Why am I like this?*

An inexplicable resistance surged up inside me. The strength of that resistance startled and even confused me.

*Why?*

I didn’t know.

For the next several hours, I failed to find the answer.

And then night fell.

[^1]: A shichen is a traditional time period of roughly two hours.
